Exp3–Exp4 · What we achieved and what to keep

2026-11-15 · Research archive and handoff

Summary

We reduced estimation error, especially player position. We did not establish a reliable match-performance advantage. Retain the Step26 player-position model as the strongest accuracy result. Keep player velocity/body as separately testable candidates. Archive ball correction as experimental because its average gain did not resolve kick-time motion errors or temporal instability.

This directory summarizes the completed work. The original Exp3/Exp4 scripts, individual steps and evidence remain at their original locations.

What we did

  1. Collected 200 baseline games with quantized 360° observations, persistent uniform numbers, no teammate payloads and reference-only fullstate.
  2. Tested geometry/reachability filters, temporal GRUs, nearby-player/ball context, uniform-number embeddings, masked neighbor pooling, range/bearing readouts and single/multiple movement hypotheses.
  3. Selected Step26 for live player-position correction: eight-cycle recurrent history, masked nearby-player context and a bounded range/bearing correction consistent with the quantized observation.
  4. Trained Exp4 models for player velocity/body, current ball position/velocity and one-cycle ball state. Tested estimated kicker identity and controlled versus free-ball forecast heads.
  5. Integrated models, audited live logs, repaired observation-history feedback and ball-path inconsistencies, and added close-range guards.
  6. Added opt-in synchronous random command priority to remove the identified receive-speed advantage. Built before/after visualization and inspected observer 9's ball failures.

Exp1 → Exp3's broad player-track mean changed 159.5 → 80.0 cm, mainly under a combined change in coverage and age distribution. That is not a neural-model gain. Fresh raw error at 10–20 m stayed essentially unchanged: 39.60 versus 39.54 cm. The model comparisons below pair the same samples.

Why we are not keeping ball correction as the default

Observer left 9's replay confirmed two failure modes:

  • At cycles 784–787, the ball was seen but motion fields were omitted at reported ranges 40.4–44.7 m. Stock velocity was invalid; the model supplied an almost horizontal velocity after player 10's kicks, while the real ball moved diagonally. Fresh motion at 788 restored the direction.
  • At cycles 789–802, range-bin transitions already caused stock position jumps. The model reduced the mean position error 0.879 → 0.706 m, but worsened four of 14 individual positions and did not eliminate jitter. At 790, coasting the previous applied state would have 0.41 m error, versus 1.65 m after the fresh correction. Other cycles favored the fresh update, so this is not an always-coast rule.

The experiment therefore does not show that ball modeling is impossible, or that all instability was introduced by the model. It shows that this model and integration are not sufficiently reliable in the important situations we inspected.

Handoff: isolate prediction during visual gaps

The next question is: given accurate information when a player is visible, how well can we estimate its position while it is outside the current view?

Start with separate 60°/120°/180° view conditions and then standard adaptive view control. Keep visual identity, motion-field availability, hearing and observation cadence explicit. With a finite view cone, visibility and update frequency are separate causes of gaps. Record actual view width and observation ages every cycle.

Before collecting data:

  1. Define and verify what “no observation noise” includes. Current server::see_noise_free removes distance quantization; bearing and motion rounding remain. Self-localization error can also affect global positions. Do not label that existing flag alone a fully noiseless observation model.
  2. Retain reference fullstate strictly for labels and evaluation. If exact visible-only measurements are used in a controlled experiment, specify that visibility mask and ensure unseen truth cannot enter model inputs.
  3. Preserve position/velocity ages, actual observations, view settings and action/context history in the logs. Test the logger before choosing which archived instrumentation to bring back.
  4. Compare stock librcsc with simple motion baselines before training. Score all eligible unseen-player cycles by gap age, distance, velocity availability and context, with whole-game splits and equal sample weights.

The 360° Step26 model required eight consecutive sightings; its normal-view replay covered only 0.93% of fresh identified observations. It is not already a solution for this next question. Exp3's gap-aware filters, motion-path and likelihood reports contain reusable negative findings; read those before repeating work.
