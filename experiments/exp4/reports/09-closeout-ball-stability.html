Exp4 step 09 · Closeout: better averages, unresolved ball dynamics

2026-11-15 · Quantized 360° observations · No runtime changes in this audit

Decision

Close this research phase with player-position denoising as the strongest deployment candidate. Do not promote the combined ball correction as a reliably better game state yet.

The player-position model consistently reduces mean error by about one third in offline and live comparisons. Adding ball models improves overall mean error after the F4 repair, but it does not reliably handle missing motion fields, kicks, quantization-bin transitions or the temporal consistency needed by decisions. The screenshots identify real examples of these gaps.

This is a deployment recommendation, not proof that ball denoising always hurts or that player denoising has established a match win-rate benefit. The existing batches do not isolate ball-on versus ball-off under an otherwise identical randomized scheduling condition.

First-15 sample, exact scope and paired metrics

Case 1 · Observer left 9, cycles 784–787

The observation is fresh, but the velocity information is absent

The observer uses a see update in every cycle of 784–802. However, its ball entries contain distance and bearing only during 784–787. Both motion fields, dist_chg and dir_chg, are absent.

This is expected under the experiment configuration. For observer 9's player type 0, ball motion fading starts at 20 m and reaches certain omission at reported distance 40 m. The server computes the omission probability from the reported noisy distance. The log reports 44.7 m at 784 and 40.4 m at 785–787. Retaining uniform numbers does not disable this ball-velocity fading; see_no_fade_all remains false.

At cycle 787 the true distance is already 38.67 m, but the quantized report is still 40.4 m, so motion fields are still omitted. At 788 the report becomes 36.6 m and a motion reading is present.

360° every cycle therefore does not mean a fresh velocity measurement every cycle. The ball is seen, while its velocity can be old or invalid.

Player 10 kicks twice; the used velocity misses the new direction

Player 10 commands a kick at 783; its sense-body kick counter increases from 21 to 22 at 784. It commands another kick at 784, and the counter increases to 23 at 785. The changed true trajectory is real, not a viewer trace artifact.

By 784, the stock ball velocity age is 10 and the field is invalid. It remains invalid at ages 11, 12 and 13 through 787. Our model nevertheless supplies and applies a velocity, giving it decision-time validity. These values are almost horizontal while truth moves diagonally upward/right in Soccer Window.

The current randomized batch has 35 scored games with all 22 logs finalized, not 200 completed games in this checkout. Five other directories were unfinished/unscored when the snapshot was frozen. Its goal margin is −0.60/game, with a 95% whole-game bootstrap interval of −1.46 to +0.29. This snapshot establishes neither a benefit nor definite harm.

The first 15 randomized games confirmed the intended command-order balance: new-charge kick blocking was 49.3% on the left versus 50.0% on the right. The previous strong receive-time asymmetry is therefore absent in that verified snapshot. Later scores should not be explained away by assuming that the already-identified ordering failure remains.

The rows above are different batches and configurations; their scores cannot isolate ball correction. A same-condition ball-on/ball-off ablation would be needed to establish that causal effect. All treatment deployments shown here were on the left, which also limits generalization.

Scope, known display issue and saved evidence

The manual dataset is still marked final=false; its gzip files have not been closed. The audit reads complete CSV lines from the available decompressed prefix, freezes the relevant rows, and does not treat the manual game as a finalized scored experiment. The scored batch above uses only finalized metadata and server results. A replay file alone is not evidence that all agent logs have closed.

The ball's colored shapes can be misleading: CirclePrinter currently emits a requested color only when its color string is empty. Nonempty circle colors are consequently omitted, and the viewer uses its default outline. The expected stock/used/truth colors were not reliably visible. Numeric coordinates, radii and the STOCK/USED/TRUTH annotations remain meaningful. This is a display-only finding, and it is left unchanged as requested. The prior parser/numeric tests did not validate styling.

The white ball is server truth; the pink ball and its pink coasting dots come from the selected agent's final WM. Those dots extrapolate ordinary current velocity with decay; they are not the neural forecast output.

Relevant implementation points, inspected without editing:

  • Server src/visualsenderplayer.cpp, sendHighBall, and visualsenderplayer.h, calcNoBallVelProb: distance-dependent motion omission.
  • librcsc world_model.cpp, localizeBall and estimateBallVelByPosDiff: position update can occur without a new valid velocity; this distant case is outside the close-range position-difference fallback.
  • librcsc ball_object.cpp, updatePos: fresh localization replaces position.
  • librcsc state_denoiser.cpp: missing stock velocity is a masked input with a zero numerical fallback, not a fresh zero-speed observation.
  • librcsc world_model.cpp, updateDenoisedState: applies predicted velocity with decision validity and restores stock observation history afterward.
  • Viewer debug_painter.cpp, drawBall: ordinary coasting visualization.
